Model assumptions and data sources

For each complete town, candidate votes per entered ballot are multiplied by that town’s 2024 ballot count × turnout scale. For unreported or partial towns, the pooled candidate-votes-per-ballot rate from complete towns is applied to their 2024 ballot counts × scale. A candidate can receive at most one vote per ballot; up to three candidates can be selected. Candidate rates are therefore not normalized to sum to 100%.

Partial towns are excluded from fitting and treated as unobserved in this scenario. This is a hypothetical reweighting, not a probability forecast or a prediction interval. Reporting towns may not represent remaining towns. At zero complete towns, no scenario is produced. A complete town requires ballots and a value (including explicit zero) for every candidate.

Comparators only control historical overlays. They do not assign a predecessor’s votes to a new candidate and do not affect the model. Changing the turnout scale changes scenario vote counts, not its relative ranking.

How this general-election scenario works

2024 calibration: the three Democratic nominees received 52,586 general votes ÷ 11,617 primary votes = 4.526642×. The three Republican nominees received 33,882 general votes ÷ 3,815 primary votes = 8.881258×. These pooled party factors apply equally to that party’s 2026 nominees; Burrows is not assigned Clarkson’s individual multiplier.

For each assumed switching town, remove S × (candidate D primary votes ÷ town D ballots) from each Democratic nominee. Sum the remaining primary votes district-wide and multiply by 4.526642 × the turnout adjustment. For each Republican, multiply actual primary votes by 8.881258 × the turnout adjustment, then add the fixed number of assumed switchers. Every assumed switcher casts one vote for Murray, one for Williams, and one for Gleason. The switchers themselves are not multiplied.

The no-switch column applies exactly the same party factors and turnout adjustment without removals or additions. For chart timing only, ordinary modeled totals are distributed across towns in proportion to their remaining 2024-turnout ballot weights; fixed switcher votes remain in their assumed towns. This allocation is not a prediction of candidate geography. At full reporting, chart endpoints equal district table totals.

The turnout adjustment uniformly scales the historical conversion factors and the 36,121-ballot reference, not the fixed switcher count. Votes for eliminated D candidates are not explicitly transferred, though historical conversion factors implicitly reflect the broader general electorate. Joe Izzo, write-ins, and Progressive primary ballots are not modeled. No winner or probability is called. One historical cycle does not validate the multipliers as a forecast.
